Company as Code

Describe your company once —
every AI agent works by your rules.

An open standard for writing down how your company works — goals, roles, processes, policies — as plain markdown files in git. Verified like code. Followed by agents.

The problem

How your company works lives in your head, in chat threads, and in wiki pages nobody updates. That was survivable when a new coworker arrived once a quarter. Now every AI agent session is a new coworker — one that shows up a hundred times a day, knowing nothing.

What you get

Agents act by your rules An agent booking your invoices follows your invoicing process — and stops where your policy says a human must approve.
Changes become reviewable "Invoices go out on the 1st now" turns into a diff you can see and approve — plan before apply, the way infrastructure teams have worked for a decade.
Nothing breaks silently Delete a role three processes depend on, and the check fails before the change lands — not three weeks later.

charta — the toolchain

charta validate .   # is the description intact? every reference must resolve
charta query .      # who owns this? what serves nothing?
charta plan .       # what would this change touch — before it happens
charta mcp .        # serve the company graph to any MCP-capable agent

"So it's just markdown with a linter?" Exactly — that's the point. You start in two hours, keep your editor and your git tooling, and can leave anytime: the description belongs to you, not to a tool. Export is git clone. 100% compatible with Google's Open Knowledge Format.

Why now

People have tried this before — BPMN, handbooks, wikis. They all died the same death: nothing read them. AI agents change the equation: they read the description on every task, follow it, and notice when reality drifts away from it. For the first time, the company handbook has a reader that never skips a page.

Install

brew install pirol-ai/tap/charta
npm i -g @pirol/charta

Start from the template company, then: charta validate .
